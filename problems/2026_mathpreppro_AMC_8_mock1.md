## 1. (Algebra)

  

**Problem:**

For any integers \(p\) and \(q\): \(p \odot q = p^2 - q^2\) and \(p \oplus q = (p-q)^2\). What is the value of \((6 \odot 2)\oplus 7\)?

  

**Answer:** 625

  

**Solution:**

1. Compute \(6 \odot 2:\; 6 \odot 2 = 6^2 - 2^2 = 36 - 4 = 32.\)

2. Compute \(32 \oplus 7:\; 32 \oplus 7 = (32 - 7)^2 = 25^2 = 625.\)

3. Therefore, \((6 \odot 2)\oplus 7 = 625.\)

## 2. (Arithmetic)

  

**Problem:**

Which of the following expressions is largest?

  

**Answer:** **\(4 + 1 + 0 + 9\)**

  

**Solution:**

1. \(4 + 1 + 0 + 9 = 14.\)

2. \(4 \times 1 + 0 + 9 = 13.\)

3. \(4 + 1 \times 0 + 9 = 13.\)

4. \(4 + 1 + 0 \times 9 = 5.\)

5. \(4 \times 1 \times 0 \times 9 = 0.\)

6. The largest is \(14\), which comes from \(4 + 1 + 0 + 9\).

**Problem:**

What is \(\dfrac{1\times2\times3\times4\times5\times6\times7}{1+2+3+4+5+6+7}\)?

  

**Answer:** 180

  

**Solution:**

## 4. (Arithmetic)

  

**Problem:**

On the ArithmaQuest competition, Nora answers 15 questions correctly, answers 10 questions incorrectly, and leaves 5 questions blank. Each correctly answered question is worth 1 point, and there are no penalties for incorrect or blank answers. What is her final score on the competition?

  

**Answer:** 15

  

**Solution:**

1. Each correct answer is worth 1 point, and there is no deduction for incorrect or blank answers.

2. Nora has 15 correct answers, so her total score is \(15 \times 1 = 15\) points.

3. Therefore, Nora’s final score is 15.

## 5. (Algebra)

  

**Problem:**

Benedict and Carly are celebrating their birthdays together. Five years ago, when Carly turned 7 years old, she received a newborn puppy as a birthday present. Today, the sum of the ages of the two children and the puppy is 31 years. How many years older than Carly is Benedict?

  

**Answer:** 2

  

**Solution:**

1. Five years ago, Carly was 7 years old while the puppy was a newborn (0 years old).

2. Now, Carly is \(7+5=12\) years old and the puppy is 5 years old.

3. Let \(B\) represent Benedict’s current age. Then \(B + 12 + 5 = 31\).

4. Solve: \(B = 31 - 17 = 14.\)

5. Difference: \(14 - 12 = 2.\)

6. Therefore, Benedict is 2 years older than Carly.

## 6. (Algebra)

  

**Problem:** The average of 5 numbers in a list is 60. The average of the first 2 numbers is 48. What is the average of the last 3 numbers?

  

**Answer:** 68

  

**Solution:**

1. Let the 5 numbers be \(a, b, c, d, e\). Their sum is:

\(a+b+c+d+e = 300\) (because \(\frac{a+b+c+d+e}{5}=60\)).

2. The average of the first 2 numbers is 48, so

\(\frac{a+b}{2}=48 \Rightarrow a+b=96\).

3. Substitute \(a+b=96\) into \(a+b+c+d+e=300\):

\(96+c+d+e=300 \Rightarrow c+d+e=204\).

4. The average of \(c,d,e\) is \(\frac{204}{3}=68\).

## 7. (Geometry)

  

**Problem:** A triangular region \(EFG\) is bounded by Pine Trail, Cedar Trail, and a north–south river. Maple Street runs east–west and is perpendicular to the river. The distance along the river from \(F\) to \(G\) is 4 km, and the distance from \(E\) straight to the river along Maple Street is 2 km. Neglect the river’s width. What is the area of triangle \(EFG\) in square kilometers?

  

**Answer:** 4

  

**Solution:**

1. The area of a triangle equals \(\frac12 \times (\text{base}) \times (\text{height})\).

2. Take base \(FG\) with length 4 km; the corresponding height is the perpendicular distance from \(E\) to the river, which is 2 km (Maple Street is perpendicular to the river).

3. Area \(= \frac12 \times 4 \times 2 = 4\) square kilometers.

## 8. (Number Theory)

  

**Problem:** Let \(M\) be a 6-digit integer like 384384, where the first three digits match the last three digits in order. Which number must always divide \(M\)?

  

**Answer:** 11

  

**Solution:**

1. Any such \(M\) can be written as

\(\overline{ABCABC} = 1000\cdot \overline{ABC} + \overline{ABC} = 1001\cdot \overline{ABC}\).

2. Since \(1001 = 7 \times 11 \times 13\), \(M\) is divisible by 11.

## 9. (Geometry)

  

**Problem:** Bridget cuts open a cardboard cube along some of its edges to form a flat shape that has an area of 24 square centimeters. What is the volume of the cube in cubic centimeters?

  

**Answer:** 8

  

**Solution:**

1. Since there are 6 faces of the cube and the total area of the net is 24, each face has area \(\frac{24}{6}=4\).

2. The side length of each face is \(\sqrt{4}=2\).

3. The volume of the cube is \(2^3=8\).

## 10. (Geometry)

  

**Problem:** Aria designs four scooter routes inside identical oval corridors, shown left to right as panels 1–4. Each gray trace shows a different route. When the routes are ordered from shortest to longest, let \(S\) be the sum of the panel numbers of the two shortest routes. What is \(S\)?

  

**Answer:** 5

  

**Solution:**

1. The route in panel 3 crosses the interior the most and includes multiple long diagonals, so it is the longest.

2. Panel 2 also has two long diagonals with curved portions; it is shorter than panel 3 but longer than the remaining two.

3. Between panels 1 and 4, straight segments are shorter than curved arcs connecting the same endpoints; panel 1 is mostly straight while panel 4 follows a long smooth curve, so \(1 < 4\).

4. Therefore, the increasing order is \(1, 4, 2, 3\).

5. The two shortest are 1 and 4; hence \(S = 1 + 4 = 5\).

## 11. (Algebra)

  

**Problem:** Cassie leaves Town E at 8:30 AM, biking to Town M at a rate of 12 mph. Brian leaves Town M at 9:00 AM, biking toward Town E at 16 mph. The distance between the towns is 62 miles. At what time in the morning do they meet?

  

**Answer:** 11:00

  

**Solution:**

1. Cassie travels for 30 minutes (i.e., 0.5 hours) before Brian starts. In that half-hour, Cassie goes \(12 \times 0.5 = 6\) miles.

2. By 9:00 AM, \(62 - 6 = 56\) miles remain between them.

3. Once Brian starts, each hour they close the gap by \(12 + 16 = 28\) miles (their combined rate).

4. The time to cover the remaining 56 miles is \(\frac{56}{28} = 2\) hours.

5. Add 2 hours to 9:00 AM to get 11:00 AM. Thus, they meet at **11:00**.

## 12. (Algebra)

  

**Problem:** When Clara added up her scores, she accidentally reversed the tens and units digits of one particular score. Which of the following values could represent the difference between her incorrect sum and the correct sum?

  

**Answer:** 45

  

**Solution:**

1. Let the original (correct) two-digit score be \(10a + b\), where \(a\) is the tens digit and \(b\) is the units digit.

2. The incorrect reversed score becomes \(10b + a\).

3. The difference between the correct and reversed score is:

\(\lvert(10a+b)-(10b+a)\rvert = \lvert 9a-9b\rvert = 9\lvert a-b\rvert\).

4. Thus, the difference must be a multiple of 9.

5. Among the given options, only 45 is a multiple of 9. Hence, the difference could be **45**.

## 13. (Arithmetic)

  

**Problem:** If Joel’s phone is left on but not used, the battery lasts for 36 hours. If it is used constantly, the battery lasts for only 4 hours. So far, the phone has been on for 10 hours, and during that time it has been used for 120 minutes. If Joel now leaves the phone on without using it, how many more hours will the battery last?

  

**Answer:** 10

  

**Solution:**

1. The phone consumes \(\frac{1}{36}\) of its battery each hour when idle and \(\frac{1}{4}\) of its battery each hour when in use.

2. Over 10 hours, 2 hours were active use (since 120 minutes is 2 hours) and 8 hours were idle.

3. The battery used is

\(2\cdot\frac{1}{4} + 8\cdot\frac{1}{36} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{8}{36} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{9} = \frac{13}{18}\).

4. Thus, \(\frac{5}{18}\) of the battery remains. If the phone is left idle, it uses \(\frac{1}{36}\) of the battery per hour.

5. Therefore, it will last

\(\frac{5/18}{1/36} = \frac{5}{18}\cdot 36 = 10\) more hours.

## 14. (Number Theory)

  

**Problem:** The smallest integer \(>1\) leaving a remainder 1 when divided by 4, 5, and 6 lies between which pair of numbers?

  

**Answer:** 60 and 79

  

**Solution:**

1. \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{4}\), \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{5}\), \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{6}\) implies that \(n-1\) is a multiple of \(\mathrm{LCM}(4,5,6)=60\).

2. Thus, \(n-1=60\), so \(n=61\), which is between 60 and 79.

## 15. (Counting and Probability)

  

**Problem:** In the country of Alphonia, all car license plates have 4 symbols. The first symbol must be a vowel (A, E, I, O, U). The second and third symbols must be distinct letters from the remaining 21 letters that are not vowels, and the fourth symbol must be a digit (0 through 9). If these symbols are chosen at random following these rules, what is the probability that the plate will read "OWL7"?

  

**Answer:** 1/21,000

  

**Solution:**

1. There is exactly 1 favorable outcome: the plate spelled exactly as "OWL7".

2. Calculate the total number of possible plates:

- The first symbol (a vowel) has 5 possible choices (A, E, I, O, U).

- The second symbol (a consonant from the 21 non-vowels) has 21 choices.

- The third symbol (another distinct consonant) has 20 choices remaining.

- The fourth symbol (a digit from 0 to 9) has 10 choices.

3. Multiply these possibilities to find the total number of license plates:

\(5 \times 21 \times 20 \times 10 = 21{,}000\).

4. Because only one of these plates is "OWL7", the probability is \(\frac{1}{21{,}000}\).

## 16. (Algebra)
**Problem:**  
A curator measured the masses of 5 rock samples arranged left to right. Each recorded mass (in kilograms) was an integer, and for every adjacent pair the mass of the sample on the left was either twice or half the mass of the one to its right. A spill smeared the sheet, but Sample 3 was still readable as 21 kilograms. The bottom of the table showed that the average mass ended with .6 kilograms. What was the average mass of the five samples, in kilograms?

**Answer:** **54.6**

**Solution (steps):**
1. Since Sample 3 = 21 is odd and all masses are integers, Sample 2 and Sample 4 must each be \(2 \times 21 = 42\) (they cannot be \(21/2\)).
2. With Sample 2 = 42 and Sample 4 = 42, Sample 1 and Sample 5 must each be either 21 (half of 42) or 84 (double of 42). The four possibilities give averages:  
   - \((21, 42, 21, 42, 21)\) → \(147/5 = 29.4\)  
   - \((21, 42, 21, 42, 84)\) → \(210/5 = 42\)  
   - \((84, 42, 21, 42, 21)\) → \(210/5 = 42\)  
   - \((84, 42, 21, 42, 84)\) → \(273/5 = 54.6\)
3. Only one average ends with .6, namely 54.6. Therefore, the average mass is **54.6**.

## 17. (Algebra)
**Problem:**  
In Mr. Finley’s class, \(\tfrac{2}{3}\) of the boys and \(\tfrac{3}{4}\) of the girls passed a certain quiz. An equal number of boys and girls passed the quiz. What is the minimum possible number of students in the class?

**Answer:** **17**

**Solution (steps):**
1. Let \(b\) = number of boys and \(g\) = number of girls.
2. Boys who passed: \(\tfrac{2}{3}b\). Girls who passed: \(\tfrac{3}{4}g\).
3. Equal passed means \(\tfrac{2}{3}b = \tfrac{3}{4}g\).
4. Solve: \(\tfrac{2}{3}b = \tfrac{3}{4}g \Rightarrow b = \tfrac{3}{2}\cdot\tfrac{3}{4}g = \tfrac{9}{8}g\).
5. For \(b,g\) to be integers, \(g\) must be a multiple of 8. Smallest is \(g=8\), giving \(b=\tfrac{9}{8}\cdot 8 = 9\).
6. Total students \(= g+b = 8+9 = 17\).

## 18. (Counting & Probability)
**Problem:**  
In a regional swimming meet, 181 swimmers enter a 200-meter freestyle race. The pool has 6 lanes, so only 6 swimmers compete at a time. After each race, the 5 non-winners are eliminated, and the winner advances to a subsequent race. How many races must be run to determine the overall champion?

**Answer:** **36**

**Solution (steps):**
1. We want to end up with 1 champion from 181 swimmers. Each race eliminates 5 swimmers, because only the winner continues.
2. Total swimmers: 181.
3. One winner remains, so \(181 - 1 = 180\) swimmers must be eliminated.
4. Each race eliminates 5 swimmers, so the number of races is \(180/5 = 36\).

## 19. (Counting & Probability)
**Problem:**  
A fair 6-sided die is rolled twice. What is the probability that the first roll is greater than or equal to the second roll?

**Answer:** **\(\tfrac{7}{12}\)**

**Solution (steps):**
1. There are \(6 \times 6 = 36\) total possible outcomes when rolling two dice.
2. Of these outcomes, 6 have the same number on both rolls (i.e., (1,1), (2,2), …, (6,6)).
3. For the remaining \(36 - 6 = 30\) outcomes, half will have the first roll larger than the second, and the other half will have the second roll larger than the first. So 15 of those show a strictly larger first roll.
4. Favorable outcomes \(= 15 + 6 = 21\).
5. Probability \(= \tfrac{21}{36} = \tfrac{7}{12}\).

## 20. (Geometry)
**Problem:**  
A game spinner has three concentric circles centered at \(H\) with radii 4, 6, and 9. Points \(M\) and \(N\) lie on the largest circle. The entire band between radii 4 and 6 is painted, and on the outer band (between radii 6 and 9) only the sector cut off by \(\angle MHN\) is painted. The small inner circle (radius 4) is unpainted. If the total painted area equals the total unpainted area, what is the measure of \(\angle MHN\) in degrees?

**Answer:** **164**

**Solution (steps):**
1. Painted area  
   \[
   \pi(6^2-4^2) + \frac{x}{360}\pi(9^2-6^2)
   = 20\pi + \frac{x}{360}\cdot 45\pi.
   \]
2. Unpainted area  
   \[
   \pi\cdot 4^2 + \left(1-\frac{x}{360}\right)\pi(9^2-6^2)
   = 16\pi + 45\pi - \frac{x}{360}\cdot 45\pi
   = 61\pi - \frac{x}{360}\cdot 45\pi.
   \]
3. Set painted = unpainted:  
   \[
   20\pi + \frac{x}{360}\cdot 45\pi
   = 61\pi - \frac{x}{360}\cdot 45\pi.
   \]
4. Combine terms:  
   \[
   \frac{x}{360}\cdot 90\pi = 41\pi \Rightarrow x=\frac{41\cdot 360}{90}=164.
   \]
   So \(\angle MHN = 164^\circ\).

## 21. (Number Theory)
**Problem:**  
A box contains silver marbles. When these marbles are equally divided among 8 friends, there are 3 marbles left over. When the same marbles are equally divided among 5 friends, there are 4 marbles left over. If the box holds the smallest number of marbles that meets these two conditions, how many marbles are left when divided equally among 7 friends?

**Answer:** **5**

**Solution (steps):**
1. We want a number \(N\) such that:  
   \(N \equiv 3 \pmod{8}\) and \(N \equiv 4 \pmod{5}\).
2. Possible values satisfying \(N \equiv 3 \pmod{8}\) are \(3, 11, 19, 27, 35, \ldots\)
3. Possible values satisfying \(N \equiv 4 \pmod{5}\) are \(4, 9, 14, 19, 24, \ldots\)
4. The smallest common value is 19.
5. Now divide 19 by 7: \(19 \div 7\) leaves a remainder of 5.  
   Therefore, there are **5** marbles left over.

## 22. (Geometry)
**Problem:**  
In a step-shaped arrangement of 3 congruent squares \(KLMN\) (bottom-left), \(LPQM\) (bottom-right), and \(RSTU\) (top), the side \(UT\) of the top square lies along the top edges of the two lower squares. Points are chosen so that \(LU=\tfrac{1}{3}KL\) and \(LT=\tfrac{2}{3}LP\). A segment is drawn from \(R\) (the top-left corner of the upper square) to \(Q\) (the bottom-right corner of the right-hand lower square). The region bounded by \(RQ\) and the outer edges \(QP, PT, TS,\) and \(SR\) is shaded. What fraction of the total area of the 3 squares is unshaded?

**Answer:** **\(\tfrac{2}{3}\)**

**Solution (steps):**
1. Let each square have side 1. Place coordinates so the bottom-left square has corners \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\); the right-bottom square extends to \((2,0)\) and \((2,1)\). The top square has  
   \(U=(\tfrac{2}{3},1),\ T=(\tfrac{5}{3},1),\ R=(\tfrac{2}{3},2),\ S=(\tfrac{5}{3},2)\).
2. Line \(RQ\) goes through \(R(\tfrac{2}{3},2)\) and \(Q(2,0)\) with slope \(-\tfrac{3}{2}\). Its intersection with \(y=1\) is \(X(\tfrac{4}{3},1)\).
3. In the top square, the shaded piece is trapezoid \(RSTX\) with bases \(RS=1\) and \(TX=\tfrac{5}{3}-\tfrac{4}{3}=\tfrac{1}{3}\) and height 1. Its area is  
   \[
   \frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}\right)\cdot 1=\frac{2}{3}.
   \]
4. In the right-bottom square, the shaded piece is right triangle \(XPQ\) with legs \(PX=2-\tfrac{4}{3}=\tfrac{2}{3}\) and \(PQ=1\). Its area is  
   \[
   \frac{1}{2}\cdot 1\cdot \frac{2}{3}=\frac{1}{3}.
   \]
5. Total shaded area \(=\tfrac{2}{3}+\tfrac{1}{3}=1\) (equal to one square). Total area \(=3\), so unshaded fraction is  
   \[
   \frac{3-1}{3}=\frac{2}{3}.
   \]

## 23. (Geometry)
**Problem:**  
Three square tiles and two rectangular tiles exactly fill a large rectangle of width 2848 units and height 1800 units, arranged as in the figure. The small square sits between a larger square on the left and another square on the right, with the two rectangles filling the remaining top and bottom gaps. What is the side length of the small central square in units?

**Answer:** **524**

**Solution (steps):**
1. Let the side lengths of the left, center, and right squares be \(a, b, c\). From the top edge of the large rectangle, the widths add to \(a+b+c=2848\).
2. From the height, the top rectangle has height \(a-b\), so \(a-b+c=1800\).
3. Subtract the second equation from the first:  
   \((a+b+c)-(a-b+c)=2848-1800 \Rightarrow 2b=1048\).
4. Thus \(b=\tfrac{1048}{2}=524\).
5. The side length of the small central square is **524** units.

## 24. (Geometry)
**Problem:**  
A rectangular aquarium measures 120 cm by 50 cm at its base and has a height of 60 cm. Initially, it is filled with water to a depth of 45 cm. A stone with a volume of \(1200\text{ cm}^3\) is carefully placed into the aquarium and is completely submerged. By how many centimeters does the water level rise?

**Answer:** **0.2**

**Solution (steps):**
1. Calculate the area of the aquarium’s base: \(120 \times 50 = 6000\text{ cm}^2\).
2. Volume displacement principle: Placing the stone in the water displaces \(1200\text{ cm}^3\) of water.
3. Determine the increase in water depth:  
   \[
   \text{Water level rise}=\frac{1200}{6000}=0.2\text{ cm}.
   \]
   Therefore, the water level increases by **0.2 cm**.

## 25. (Geometry)
**Problem:**  
Marco cuts a cardboard display shaped like two joined rooftops. One peak is 9 centimeters high and the other is 15 centimeters high. Each peak forms a \(90^\circ\) angle, and the slanted sides make a \(45^\circ\) angle with the base. The total area of the display is 270 square centimeters. The sides cross at a valley \(h\) centimeters above the base. What is \(h\)?

**Answer:** **6**

**Solution (steps):**
1. For a right isosceles triangle whose sides meet the base at \(45^\circ\), if the peak is at height \(t\), each leg has length \(t\sqrt{2}\), so the area is  
   \[
   \frac{1}{2}(t\sqrt{2})^2=t^2.
   \]
2. The two rooftop triangles have areas \(9^2=81\) and \(15^2=225\). Their overlap is another such triangle with area \(h^2\).
3. Total area equals sum minus overlap:  
   \[
   81+225-h^2=270 \Rightarrow 306-h^2=270 \Rightarrow h^2=36 \Rightarrow h=6.
   \]
